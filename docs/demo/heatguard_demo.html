Extra checks after the locked run · exploratory, they cannot change the verdict
  • Dubai's own Heat Index chart. Misses 8 of 720, the same as HeatGuard, but warns on 93% of working minutes. HeatGuard is as safe with 69% fewer warning minutes. It is not safer.
  • Alarm burden. HeatGuard warns on 28% of working minutes (WBGT 86%). 14% of its warning episodes are followed by an event, so most warnings are still precautionary.
  • Which inputs matter. Without the wearable the model misses 56 events instead of 8. Without the pace and activity inputs it misses 42. Without weather inputs, 6.
  • Stress test. People half as different: gap closed (3 missed, WBGT 1). People half again as different: partly closed (23 missed, WBGT 11), still 68% fewer warning minutes.
  • Other random seeds. Three smaller repeats: gap closed twice, partly closed once (5 missed against 1). 66 to 76% fewer warning minutes in all three.
  • Sensor faults. No event was lost, but a fault adds a median 39 to 42 warning minutes and far more on some shifts. Reported as a defect, not fixed, because the model was locked.
  • Field averages. The simulated workers match the study they were calibrated to and run hotter and harder than the Qatar and UAE field studies.
  • Speed. One decision takes a median 12 ms in the Python engine, against a budget of one per minute.

Simulated workers (ISO 7933 body model with person-to-person differences and self-pacing, calibrated to published field averages) on real ERA5 Dubai weather. Model trained on 200 workers in summer 2025 and tested once on 100 new workers in summer 2024 under a plan written down beforehand. The engine in this page is a JavaScript port of the Python engine; an automated check confirms the two give the same warning on every minute of every shift shown here (risk threshold 0.32, 30-minute hold, Heat Index 54 ceiling).
